Incoming PhD · University of Strasbourg · September 2026

I build multimodal models that read chest radiographs and write reports a clinician can trace back to the pixels.

I am an MS by Research candidate at IIIT Hyderabad, working in the Cognitive Science Lab and IHub-Data with Prof. Raju S. Bapi and Dr. Tapabrata Chakraborty (UCL, Alan Turing Institute), in clinical collaboration with NIMS Hospital, Hyderabad. My thesis develops vision-language models for grounded radiology report generation — systems whose every stated finding is anchored to visual evidence and to a clinician-verified label.

From September 2026 I join the University of Strasbourg for a PhD with Prof. Caroline Essert and Dr. Juan Verde, extending this work from diagnosis to intervention: LLM-based multimodal planning and decision support for pulmonary tumour ablation.

Research

Grounded radiology report generation

My MS thesis work builds multimodal vision-language models for chest X-ray interpretation where findings are spatially grounded and separately evaluated for hallucination, so a radiologist can check a sentence against the region that produced it rather than against the model's confidence.

VLMsChest X-ray

Clinician-in-the-loop datasets and benchmarks

Indian clinical imaging is under-represented in public benchmarks. Working with NIMS Hospital, I build large-scale image–text datasets with structured expert annotation protocols, alongside an image quality assessment and quality control framework that decides which studies are fit to train and evaluate on.

Dataset developmentStructured annotationIQA / QC

From diagnosis to intervention

Starting with my PhD: image-guided surgical planning for pulmonary tumour ablation, where multimodal models must reason over anatomy geometrically and defend a plan to the clinician who will execute it.

Surgical planningGeometric reasoningDecision support

Longer term I am interested in multimodal reasoning, world models, and scientific AI — foundation models that support biomedical discovery rather than only classifying what a clinician has already seen.
